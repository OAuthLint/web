Disclosure policy

Last updated 2026-09-01

We want to hear about any vulnerability in OAuthLint, whether it is in the CLI, the rules, the app or the service we run. If you report in good faith and follow this policy, we will work with you, fix it quickly, and not pursue legal action.

Report a vulnerability

Email security@auspeo.dev. Do not open a public issue.

Email the security team

Scope

Everything we publish or operate:

  • The OAuthLint CLI and the rule pack
  • The editor extensions and the CI integration
  • The app, self-hosted or on our Cloud
  • The source-control integrations for GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket and Azure DevOps
  • oauthlint.dev and its subdomains

A rule that misses a real bug, or flags correct code, is a detection issue rather than a vulnerability. Report it as a false negative or false positive on the rule's page.

How to report

Send one email per issue. The more of this you can include, the faster we can confirm it:

  • The component and version affected
  • Steps to reproduce, or a proof of concept
  • What an attacker could do with it
  • Whether you have shared it with anyone else

Please give us a reasonable time to fix the issue before you disclose it publicly. We will agree a date with you.

What happens next

We confirm we received your report, assess it, and keep you updated until it is fixed. Our targets:

Response and fix targets by severity
SeverityFirst responseFix target
Critical1 business day7 days
High1 business day30 days
Medium3 business days90 days
Low5 business daysNext release

Fixes to the Cloud ship as soon as they are ready. Self-hosted fixes ship as a release with a security advisory, so you know when to update.

Safe harbour

We will not pursue or support legal action against you for research that follows this policy. That means you:

  • Test only against your own account, or a self-hosted instance you run
  • Stop and report as soon as you reach data that is not yours
  • Do not degrade the service for other users
  • Keep the details private until the issue is fixed

Out of scope

  • Denial of service and volumetric attacks
  • Social engineering and physical attacks
  • Reports from automated scanners with no demonstrated impact
  • Missing security headers with no exploitable consequence
  • Vulnerabilities in a third-party service we do not control

Recognition

With your permission, we credit you in the security advisory and on this page. We do not run a paid bounty programme today.
